Quiet Mirror Style Guide

Locked visual and editing system so every video feels intentional and consistent.

Character system

Primary figure: large round head, light beige shirt, dark trousers, simple black shoes, thin imperfect ink lines.

Shadow self: same figure, darker olive or charcoal shirt, slightly slumped. Appears only in internal conflict.

Helper hand: soft outlined hand or arm that approaches but stops short unless the script allows contact.

Color language

Beige shirt = present self
Olive / charcoal = shadow or protective pattern
Terracotta accent = emotional heat or decision point
Soft paper background with faint grain — never pure white, never busy environments.

Master prompt prefix

hand-drawn 2D stick-figure illustration, large round-headed young adult character, thin slightly imperfect ink outlines, simple facial features, expressive body language, warm muted earth-tone palette, subtle paper grain, flat illustrated environments or almost empty frame, intelligent editorial aesthetic, cinematic but quiet composition, soft atmospheric lighting, emotionally meaningful pose, no text, no photorealism, no 3D, no anime

Editing approach

Stills + light motion only. No full character animation required.

  • One still per scene / idea
  • Hold 5–8 seconds
  • Add slow 102–105% scale or gentle drift
  • Hard cut on the voiceover beat
  • Status-flip: clean cut from shadow figure to primary figure on the same composition
  • Almost-touch hands held a beat longer than comfortable
  • Music very low or none; subtle paper or breath texture only
  • Captions: one short clause, large, bottom third

Niche Library

The main lanes under POV Explainer and how the Quiet Mirror style adapts to each.

Rank / Level Progression

Master POV style. “Your life at every level of [career, status, underworld, military].” 15–30 min, slow build with sensory detail and the emotional cost of each rank.

Style shift: More physical sensory detail per level. Shadow figure appears at the price of each rank. Longer holds on the cost moments.

Psychology / Internal POV

PsychToons and Mind’s Eye style. Explaining a private behavior, bias, or emotional pattern from inside it. 8–16 min, calm, metaphor-heavy reframe.

Style shift: Heavier metaphor props (mirror, clenched fist, closed door). Shadow figure more present. The status-flip cut is the key edit.

Scenario / Role Immersion

Mr. POV Explainer style. “POV: You are [extreme role]” — cartel accountant, deep cover, black-hat, evidence destroyer. 10–20 min, tension + process.

Style shift: Process-focused (hands doing the work). Fewer emotional metaphors. Keep the quiet pacing so it doesn’t feel like generic action stickman content.

Status / Everyday Journey

Climbing fame, money, skill, or social rank with the psychological cost attached. Medium-long and reflective.

Style shift: Mix of rank structure + psychology layer. Shadow figure tracks the internal price of external progress.

Dark / Warning POV

Dark psychology, manipulation, or toxic patterns explained from the inside. Shorter to medium, cautionary. Use sparingly.

Style shift: Shadow figure dominates longer. Terracotta used sparingly for the decision point. Keep the educational disclaimer clear.

Recommended starting structure

Central concept: Experience it. Understand it.

Pillar 1 — Human Psychology (≈50%)
POV: You Always Feel Guilty When You Say No. / You Would Rather Struggle Than Ask for Help.

Pillar 2 — Power, Work & Money (≈30%)
POV: You Become the Most Competent Person Nobody Notices. / Promotion changes how people treat you.

Pillar 3 — Hypothetical Worlds (≈20%)
Only scenarios that still explore behavior or status (social scores, AI taking jobs, money becoming worthless).

Start here. Test 6–9 videos across these three related pillars before expanding further. Keep narration, visual style, and structure consistent.
